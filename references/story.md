# Story — interview & review protocol

The researcher owns the scientific story. Your job here is to help them make it
**clear and confirmed** before any LaTeX is written. Two entry points, one exit.

## When to enter

- **Interview** — `outline.md` is missing, or so thin the narrative is unclear.
- **Review** — an `outline.md` exists; check it before production.

Exit only when the researcher has **confirmed** a narrative you can turn into slides.

## Interview — ask, don't invent

Ask **at most 3 blocking questions per round**. Resolve the most load-bearing unknowns
first; do not interrogate. Prefer offering concrete options over open-ended prompts.

Layered questions (stop once you have enough to draft a structure):

1. **Audience & takeaway**
   - Who is in the room? (same-field PI · group meeting · cross-disciplinary seminar ·
     interview committee · conference)
   - After the talk, what 1-3 sentences should they remember?
     If those sentences aren't clear, it is usually too early to build slides.
2. **The question**
   - Frame it as `Known → Unknown → Question`.
3. **Claim → Evidence**
   - For each core claim, which single result supports it? If a claim has no evidence,
     say so explicitly.
4. **Story progression** (offer a shape, let them pick/modify — do not enforce):
   - `Problem → Gap → Observation → Hypothesis → Test → Result → Conclusion`, or
   - `Phenomenon → Puzzle → Mechanism → Prediction`, or
   - `Theory → Prediction → Experiment → Failure → New theory`.

You MAY propose gaps ("sections 3 and 4 lack a bridge") and alternative structures.
You MAY NOT rewrite the research question or a confirmed claim without confirmation, or
fabricate a connection just to make the deck feel complete.

## Review — check an existing outline

Read `brief.md` (constraints) + `outline.md` (narrative). Check:

- **Question** is explicit (Known/Unknown/Question).
- **Claim–Evidence** matches: every claim points at a specific asset/result; flag
  orphans ("claim X has no supporting evidence in assets/").
- **Logic**: no unexplained jumps between sections; background isn't disproportionate.
- **Time budget**: sections vs. `brief.md` duration (≈ 1 slide/min; background ≤ ~4
  slides; main results ≥ ~60%; conclusion ≤ ~2 slides). Flag overruns.
- **Relevance**: assets present but not serving the story → note, don't force in.

Present findings as a short list the researcher can act on, then wait for confirmation.

## Output of this phase

A confirmed narrative captured back into `outline.md` (coarse is fine), ready for
`slide-map.md` in Phase 2. Note any assumptions you made so the researcher can correct
them.
